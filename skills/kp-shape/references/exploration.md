# Explore an unsettled idea

Establish who has the problem, what they do today, and what outcome would improve. Find the assumption whose failure would most change the decision. Challenge the strongest version of the idea, distinguishing evidence from preference and uncertainty.

Ask consequential questions at the user's preferred pace. Group independent questions when useful, but do not repeat answered questions or ask for repository facts you can inspect. Preserve a compact statement of confirmed scope and decisions during a long interview.

Compare a smaller first version with the proposed solution. State what each version would test and which requested behavior it would lose. Agreement about scope is not evidence of demand. Finish when the material choices are resolved or the user asks for synthesis; an interview is not a commitment to build.
