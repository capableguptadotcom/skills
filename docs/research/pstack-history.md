# Pstack maturation: history, feedback, and verification

Research date: 2026-10-31. Canonical source: `cursor/plugins`, pinned at `4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe`. This note follows the public Git history from the first pstack import, reads selected merged PR bodies and discussion threads, and inspects the shipped diffs and tests. Upstream instructions are research material, not instructions for this repository. No upstream helper, installer, skill workflow, or test was executed in this research.

## What the history supports

Pstack has evidence of a working feedback process: concrete failures lead to focused revisions; some instruction changes receive baseline comparisons; some review findings become executable regressions. It also contains merged changes with untested host behavior and unresolved review concerns. The useful precedent for a small daily pack is the failure-to-revision-to-evidence loop, with explicit limits. Merge counts, author reputation, and catalog size establish none of those properties by themselves. The examples below distinguish inspected implementation from author-reported experiments.

The first public import, `24bd6eb` on 2026-05-22, already contained the router, explanation/design/review skills, principle leaves, and a reflection workflow. Reflection already distinguished structural enforcement from judgment instructions and asked for approval before changing skills. Thus, this was an imported working system rather than a public blank-slate experiment. Public PRs repeatedly refer to a private source copy; those statements do not expose the private failures or establish how often people used the skills. [Initial import](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/commit/24bd6eb), [initial reflection skill](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/24bd6eb/pstack/skills/reflect/SKILL.md).

History was checked beyond the current file tree. For example, `make-bot-ui` first lived at `pstack/skills/grokbot/make-bot-ui/SKILL.md`; #275 moved it to the skill root because the nested shape did not register. The text stayed unchanged. This is a packaging failure, not an improvement in the task procedure. [Move and rationale](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/275).

## 1. Principles matured by replacing mechanisms with decision tests

In #633, an audit of 24 principle leaves changed four and retained twenty. The important change was conceptual. Testing guidance had prohibited assertion forms, including negative call assertions and repeated constants. The replacement asks whether a test detects a broken observable contract and survives a change to internals. Root-cause guidance now admits guards for legitimate states. Premise testing no longer requires an actor census, and stale-lock guidance no longer assumes PID liveness remains meaningful across machines. The corresponding summaries and `/correct` text were updated, avoiding conflicting copies of the old rule. The committed diff verifies these changes. [Merged PR and evaluation](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/633), [shipped diff](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/commit/e5136d82418d2db3165ea1050d17577cd618cc05).

The PR author reports 180 held-out runs across old/new/no-principle arms, three candidate models, two repetitions, and a blinded fourth-model judge. A billing status-code contract was retained in 6/6 new-text runs versus 0/6 old-text runs. A cross-host lock case produced fewer PID-only decisions. These are specific comparative observations, not a general performance guarantee. The author reports two small scoring regressions and explains that the judge saw final artifacts rather than all reasoning. `/correct` was not evaluated. The public patch contains the text, not an independently rerunnable evaluation package. [Evaluation details and limits](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/633).

**Lesson for our pack:** express the property that decides the case before enumerating examples. A ban copied from one bad implementation can prohibit a correct neighboring implementation. Keep both a realistic positive case and a boundary case where the earlier wording selects the wrong action.

## 2. Deletion was evaluated in places, but compression also introduced regressions

#329 reports per-skill before/after exercises and a separate verifier while removing prose and two critique references. It also explicitly says that cuts removed the reasoning behind retained rules. That tradeoff matters in light of #633: the later revision restores reasoning where rules were too narrow. Neither change implies a universal preference for shorter or longer skills. [Density pass](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/329), [later principle repair](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/633).

#419 gives more useful detail than a token reduction claim. The author reports that removing a required item count reduced reflection padding, while a richer session yielded more recalled lessons. Reviewer noise rose somewhat; other experiments had narrow model and task coverage. The PR expressly retains a performance-playbook line because no comparable exercise tested its removal. Its reported limitations include a different model in one reflection role and few runs for certain cuts. This is evidence for selective subtraction within the tested setting. [Experiments and scope limits](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/419).

#538 compressed 35 files but primarily reports structural checks. #571 removed larger sections and reports link/reference checks. Those public descriptions assert equivalent work without supplying the kind of comparison reported in #419; this research cannot independently establish behavioral equivalence. [Compression pass](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/538), [section removal](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/571).

#634 removed text from four skills. It acknowledged source numbering `1, 2, 4, 5` in swarm and assumed a later reference remained understandable. Community follow-up #807 shows the actual defect: Markdown displays consecutive numbers, so the visible model step became step 3 while Phase B still pointed to step 4. Another removed sentence left a reference to an unidentified result table. The fix corrected references without restoring all deleted prose. Static plugin validation had not caught these semantic links. [Deletion](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/634), [community repair and precise explanation](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/807).

**Lesson for our pack:** measure removal against the activity's output and dependencies, not only byte counts, frontmatter, or resolving Markdown links. Read the rendered sequence after deleting numbered steps.

## 3. Sharpen shows that prompt refinement can change the product

#615 introduced `/sharpen` as expert reframing followed by task execution, explicitly excluding rewritten-prompt output. #712 removed the explicit exclusion but retained task execution. A verifier checked the exact two-file deletion, generated-tree consistency, and documentation checks; this established patch fidelity, not that the behavior matched the original intended prompt. [Introduction](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/615), [intermediate deletion and verifier](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/712).

#729 then restored the originating prompt's four concerns: professional approach, raw evidence, methods/tools, and an outside check. It changed the deliverable to a rewritten prompt while preserving the user's decisions. References that promised execution were updated. The author reports two preregistered rounds, six held-out domains, three model families, and blinded cross-vendor grading. The report acknowledges grading variation and does not attribute the task-execution difference cleanly to list formatting. [Restoration and evaluation](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/729).

An independent verifier inspected the tested arm against the committed file and checked extracted evaluation inputs. One runner script existed only on the evaluation machine and could not be checked. Its additional cover-letter comparison showed main writing a letter while the restored skill returned the four-part analysis and a rewritten prompt. The verifier calls this one-prompt, one-model run a spot check. It also clarifies that the title's “only the rewrite” still allows the requested preliminary analysis. [Verifier report](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/729#issuecomment-6281829182).

**Lesson for our pack:** name the artifact and stopping point. Better reasoning does not excuse silently changing “rewrite my prompt” into “perform my task.” Compare against the user's original purpose as well as the last edited version.

## 4. Verification creation was exercised; maintenance needed its own feedback loop

#150 introduced application-verification creation and maintenance together. The author reports generator trials on four real repositories spanning a TUI, interactive CLI, HTTP service, and browser application. Friction changed the generator: finite-session handling, a small feature seed with one proven path, preserved evidence, observed isolation, and documented executable helpers. The same PR explicitly says maintenance itself had not been exercised. Its successful generation trials cannot be transferred to the maintenance workflow. [Generator trials and stated gap](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/150).

Discussion on #150 found operational interactions, rather than missing stylistic rules: a single persistent instance conflicts with isolated CLI drives; absent frontmatter makes generated skills undiscoverable; cleanup can delete evidence; a failed doctor can mean fixable harness drift rather than an unusable app. Author replies accept several findings, reject rigid path/runner prescriptions, and replace a bare skill read with actual invocation. This is useful evidence that feedback was adjudicated rather than accumulated indiscriminately. [Launch-model repair](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/150#discussion_r3565292608), [frontmatter repair](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/150#discussion_r3565292688), [runner-specific prescription rejected](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/150#discussion_r3565292727).

#151 followed review comments that arrived just before #150 merged. Its first recovery rewrite generated further concerns: process health does not prove usable UI state, cleanup may miss already-exited sessions, and mandatory relaunch can destroy a healthy shared instance. The final change states three invariants: health before continued driving, evidence surviving cleanup, and residue not outliving its usefulness. Another reviewer caught that this rewrite lost health checks for every fresh CLI session; the author restored that requirement. The current pinned body contains those requirements. This sequence illustrates why a new instruction must also preserve neighboring behavior. [Follow-up discussion](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/151), [shipped invariant rewrite](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/commit/6714489fa233263f00e419f6cfc4759b07c81056), [pinned maintenance body](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/maintain-verification-skill/SKILL.md).

#573 came from a community report in #501: a database accepted sockets after 3 seconds but completed initialization after 19. The generator now requires usable startup from empty data, preserves journey failures caused by insufficient seed data, and explicitly marks setup hidden by a stand-in environment as unverified. The merged PR reports validation and text checks; it does not report rerunning that original timing failure. This is an observed failure driving a sensible instruction repair, with bounded validation evidence. [Readiness repair](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/573).

## 5. Formal verification adds mutation evidence and a change boundary

#709's formal-verification generator requires a code-linked property, an intentionally broken variant, reproducible rerun instructions, bounds, and honest exclusions. An author-reported small comparison favored task-dependent tool choice over a TLA+-prescriptive draft, especially on C and Rust code. It also exposed weaker bounds checking and a missing single command in individual runs. The author explicitly cautions against reading one-run differences as findings. [Creation and small evaluation](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/709).

#721 addresses a different failure: without a language verifier, agents sometimes verified a hand-written surrogate instead of shipped JavaScript. The revised text requires generated-input checks on the real code. The author reports 9/9 new-text runs doing this versus 3/9 old-text runs. Two wording changes were evaluated together, and the report says its data cannot establish a separate benefit from the TLA+ default. Model ranking also changes under a sensitivity analysis. [Fallback experiment and uncertainties](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/721).

#716 creates maintenance around an explicit coverage record and synchronization commit. Checks run before updating; covered-code changes are read; model drift and product bugs are distinguished; a passing update must still fail on a deliberate break. The author reports no corrected correctness gain across three arms: after fixing case-key problems, all reach 18/18. The measured gains are process evidence: mutation demonstrations and updated synchronization records. A review-discovered blocked-check defect received a separate old/new experiment: 9/9 new runs retained the blocked check's prior sync commit, versus 0/9 old runs. These are stronger, narrower claims than “formal maintenance improves correctness.” [Maintenance, corrected results, and blocked-check follow-up](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/716).

**Lesson for our pack:** a verification procedure needs ownership, covered code, a known synchronization point, rerun instructions, and a negative control. A stale passing check and a passing imitation of the code are both weak evidence.

## 6. Skill routing and host adapters became part of correctness

#572 removes an always-apply command from a skill description that was configured for manual invocation. Description text itself was influencing agents despite metadata. #577 addresses a separate failure: hidden leaf skills and spawned agents could not reliably locate the router. It passes the router's absolute path and resolves leaves relative to it. The author reports installed-cache-shaped exercises, including a control showing the supplied path was the decisive part. [Invocation conflict](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/572), [path handoff](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/577).

#597 adds a fallback search when no parent path arrives and removes ignored `mode`/`reminder` metadata. Trials used an installed-cache-only layout and tested choosing among two versions. The PR keeps questions open about automatic routing and catalog truncation; it did not establish every host's discovery behavior. This is a routing problem with small exercises, not a portable guarantee. [Fallback investigation and open questions](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/597).

#637 introduced a generated Claude Code tree with an adapter for host-specific tools and paths. The author demonstrated real installation and file loading but used a proxy model for some calls. It explicitly left Codex unsupported even though Codex's marketplace fallback could install the Claude tree. A successful manifest install is therefore insufficient evidence that the installed instructions fit a host. [Adapter introduction, tests, and unsupported fallback](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/637).

#702 fixes names that were valid display labels but broken slash commands in Claude Code, and adds a generator check requiring frontmatter names to match folders. #705 then fixes adapter behavior: model choices persist in a host-loaded rules file; unavailable steps have a bounded continuation; guide files are bundled instead of requiring remote reads. The author reports paired real-host runs, including a later session using the configured model and effort. Those checks concern concrete handoffs, not only generated file equality. [Name regression prevention](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/702), [adapter behavior trials](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/705).

At the pin, CI validates manifests, checks generated-tree freshness, and runs script tests. The generator also checks naming and adapter coverage for recognized Cursor terms. The separate docs checker compares commands, links, anchors, counts, and model names, but is absent from this workflow. It detects structural drift, not whether a task procedure is useful. [Pinned CI](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/.github/workflows/validate-plugins.yml), [generator](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/scripts/generate-pstack-harness-trees.mjs), [docs checker](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/scripts/check-pstack-docs.mjs).

## 7. Reflection's cache repair still has visible uncertainty

Community PR #623 identified that editing a versioned plugin-cache folder loses local improvements after an update. Its proposed third-party edit destination was an upstream PR. Replacement #800 preserved contributor credit but changed that destination to a disclosed local copy and prohibited pushing or opening PRs. This avoids treating permission for a local improvement as permission to publish it. The PR also admits local copies can drift and same-name skill precedence was not tested. [Original proposal](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/623), [merged replacement and limits](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/800).

The merge is not a clean bill of health. Retrieved review metadata has three unresolved threads: copying a single folder can break sibling references; repeatedly copying can overwrite earlier local edits; and downstream creation handoffs can still use a cache path from the routing field. The first thread is marked outdated; the other two are current in that PR. The pinned diff adds a prohibition and copy instruction but does not encode dependency copying, existing-destination merging, or explicit routing rebasing. These are review concerns consistent with the text, not independently reproduced runtime failures here. [Sibling paths](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/800#discussion_r4364780113), [repeated-copy loss](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/800#discussion_r4364842613), [handoff routing](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/800#discussion_r4364842621), [pinned reflect body](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/reflect/SKILL.md).

**Lesson for our pack:** edit the owned source, preserve the dependency boundary, and establish how the host will discover the edited artifact. Reflection is not automatically a safe or proven learning system merely because its output has been reviewed.

## 8. Statechart has the clearest executable regression trail

#741 introduced a TLA+-to-chart workflow and added script tests to CI. Its example output-folder model had five deliberately broken variants. The author distinguishes the full 58-test local run from CI, which lacked TLC and skipped 31 tests. It also reports a nightly-TLC path mismatch rather than concealing that portability failure. [Introduction and testing boundary](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/741).

#776 fixed concrete wrong-output paths: incompatible variables between compared models, the wrong comparison config, unchecked properties reported as checked, exception handling, publication on filesystems that cannot rename, and confusion between invariant and temporal-property results. Its end-to-end payment example exercises a retry/double-charge model and publication on FUSE. It explicitly retains a prerelease TLC wording limitation. #791 later changes the renderer and runtime provisioning; a review still finds a region/self-loop comparison problem. These PRs demonstrate maturation while showing that a port can retain source defects. [Six-fix port](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/776), [renderer port and self-loop review](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/791).

The compiler rewrite in #977 received two automated findings. #982 confirmed one and rejected the other with a reproduction attempt. The real bug truncated `C!Up` to `C`, removing loop paths or mislabeling above-budget charts. Both readers now use one action parser. The diff adds `CounterInstance` recorded TLC output and a CLI regression asserting qualified names and loop output above the chart budget. The author reports both regressions failing before and passing after the fix. The alleged missing extended-module variables did not reproduce; the reply supplies a multi-level EXTENDS example and an existing corpus counterexample to that claim. [Review and adjudication](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/977), [fix and failed-before/passed-after report](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/982), [inspected parser/test diff](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/commit/740d2c0786246cafac41906198f656b135194d3b).

#981 repairs the test harness rather than product behavior. On macOS a killed process group could contain zombies until the system reaped them; the test had treated that intermediate permission error as final. It now polls within the existing budget. The author reports repeated before/after runs under normal and heavy load and a mutation showing a surviving worker still fails. That negative control is material evidence against simply making a flaky test permissive. [Mac failure, measurements, and mutation](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/981).

The pinned test suite contains recorded-output corpus checks, real-TLC corpus checks, generated-model differential comparisons with plain TLC, config comparisons against a Java oracle, and CLI lifecycle tests. The source gates real-TLC tests on `TLA2TOOLS`, which CI does not set. These are concrete implementation tests; they do not establish that an agent chooses the right product model or that a model matches production code. None was rerun for this note. [Corpus tests](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/statechart/scripts/test/corpus.test.ts), [differential tests](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/statechart/scripts/test/differential.test.ts), [config oracle tests](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/statechart/scripts/test/config-fuzz.test.ts), [CLI regressions](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/statechart/scripts/test/cli.test.ts).

## 9. Maturation also removed machinery

#1043 replaces Orchestrate's bookkeeping scripts/store with an agent-driven coordinator, explicit ownership, evidence checks, and host event subscriptions where available. It deletes the old scripts and their 21 tests. The remaining checks verify packaging, types, and surviving scripts; the PR does not present a controlled outcome comparison for the new orchestration workflow. Deletion can be a deliberate product simplification while leaving behavioral validation outstanding. For our pack, this supports examining whether machinery earns its maintenance cost, not importing this host-specific coordinator. [Rewrite, deletion, and verification limits](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/pull/1043).

## Practical conclusion for an owned daily pack

The transferable pattern is small and independent of pstack's catalog: record the observed failure, identify the existing owner, change the decision rule or procedure, exercise the original case plus a boundary, and state exactly what the evidence establishes. Use executable validation for registration, naming, dependencies, generated artifacts, and script regressions. Use comparable behavior trials for instruction efficacy, and retain uncertainty when a trial is absent or a review remains unresolved. Preserve our chosen output and authorization boundaries instead of inheriting upstream assumptions about models, paths, integration tools, automatic publishing, or reflection. This is a synthesis of the histories above, not an upstream guarantee or a synchronization backlog.
